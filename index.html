Phone puzzle-solving robot

A two-axis gantry that watches a phone screen through a camera, solves the puzzle in real time with computer vision, and taps the answer in with a solenoid. I designed, built, and programmed it on my own, and took it through three prototypes to get it fast and accurate enough to keep up with the game.

A Python program reads the screen with OpenCV and works out the solution, then sends move commands over serial to a microcontroller that drives the stepper motors and the tapper.

How the design changed

RevA

The first proof of concept: steel linear rails screwed to a wood and MDF base, belt-driven by two stepper motors, with an Arduino running the motion and a stylus arm to touch the screen. The goal was just to prove the gantry could hit points on the phone reliably.

What I learned The wood frame flexed and shifted, so positions drifted between taps and I had to re-home constantly. It worked well enough to show the idea was possible, but not accurate enough to play the game.
RevB

Rebuilt on aluminum extrusion for a much stiffer frame, and swapped the stylus for a solenoid plunger so a tap is a quick, repeatable electrical pulse instead of a mechanical motion.

What I learned The frame was rigid, but the off-the-shelf extrusion parts made it bulky and hard to align with the phone, and the loose wiring caused intermittent faults. I wanted a compact design built around the phone itself.
RevC

A full redesign in SolidWorks. Custom 3D-printed brackets and carriages ride on linear rails, the power supplies and electronics are packaged inside the frame, and a printed phone cradle holds the phone in a fixed position. Fiducial markers on the cradle let the camera locate the screen precisely.

What changed Designing every part myself meant I could control the fit between parts, keep the whole machine compact, and make it fast and accurate enough to solve the puzzle in real time.
